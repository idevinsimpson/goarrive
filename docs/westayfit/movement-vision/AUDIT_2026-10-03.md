Prepared October 3, 2026 by ChatGPT in Devin's dedicated rep-counter thread.

## Conclusion

WE STAY FIT has an isolated, testable squat-counting research core. It does not yet have demonstrated real-person counting accuracy or a camera feature ready for the member app, shared station, or native release. The highest-value next milestone is a scored human session on an exact, isolated HTTPS candidate. More synthetic passes cannot substitute for that session.

This audit reproduced camera ownership defects, implemented a bounded successor, strengthened misleading proof checks, and defined the smallest eventual app integration. The successor is source and focused-test work. It is not an exported app, hosted preview, human-device pass, or release acceptance. The original accepted research snapshot remains intact.

## What the accepted counter actually does

The pure pipeline is `PoseFrame` to `MovementSession` to `SessionSnapshot`. A browser adapter supplies local pose landmarks. The session selects a spatially continuous subject, calibrates standing geometry, and counts a complete observed standing/down/standing cycle. The current experiment supports squats only.

Its default down threshold is 0.6 and upright threshold 0.25, with 100 ms dwell and a 400 ms minimum interval between counted repetitions. These are algorithm parameters, not validated accuracy or a medical/form standard. The written squat-depth instruction and the operational threshold must be reconciled before human scoring.

The accepted core refuses overlapping detected people, invalidates an unobserved partial cycle, rejects non-increasing timestamps, and voids a partial cycle across a processed-frame gap greater than 250 ms. At exactly 250 ms the existing boundary permits continuity. Reacquisition is based on position/size and time; it does not authenticate identity. A person replacing another at the same location can satisfy those geometric conditions.

The browser adapter pins `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` to 0.10.35 and runs a lite pose model with up to three detections. The actual imported vendor bundle is CommonJS. The native component is a manual-only placeholder. No direct frame recording, image upload, raw-pose persistence, Firebase write, or authoritative contribution call was found in the inspected movement source.

## Reproduced defects and successor repair

### Camera startup could outlive the action that cancelled it

The original web lab awaited camera permission and pose-model initialization without checking whether the same run still owned the camera after each await. Stop, manual fallback, source changes, and unmount only stopped resources already known at that instant. Later promise resolution could reacquire resources and restart the loop.

The audit executed the actual component source with mocked hooks, DOM, camera/model promises, and animation scheduling. Five desired-behavior assertions failed on the accepted source:

1. Granting a pending permission after Stop reopened the camera, with zero track stops and one scheduled loop.
2. Granting permission after manual fallback overwrote the manual UI with camera state.
3. Granting permission after unmount accessed a null video element and leaked the stream.
4. Completing model initialization after unmount failed to close the late estimator and scheduled a loop.
5. Two pending starts followed by Stop left the earlier stream and an orphan loop alive.

The successor introduces one lifecycle owner actually used by the web component. Each start owns a distinct run. Cancelling invalidates ownership before resource cleanup; late streams and estimators release themselves without touching a newer run. The same ownership controls synthetic loops. Camera, playback, model, inference, and track-end failures terminate the current run and keep manual entry available. Route focus loss, page hiding and page exit stop the run; returning does not automatically restart it. Focus cleanup matters because navigating to another Expo route can leave the previous component mounted. Starts while unfocused or hidden are refused.

The same five independent component assertions pass on the final successor. Six additional route/page cases fail on the accepted source and pass on the successor. Three error/manual-control checks change from two passes to three: permission and model failures are preservation controls, while inference-failure cleanup discriminates the repair. The full component instrument therefore reports 2/14 on accepted source and 14/14 on the successor; the original five remain a separate group. The portable instrument is [qa/lifecycle-component-regression.cjs](qa/lifecycle-component-regression.cjs). Its proof remains a component execution with mocks, not a mounted React, physical camera or browser permission test.

The counting arithmetic, subject-lock thresholds, 250 ms continuity rule, native placeholder, package pin, and production contribution flow are not changed by this repair.

### Existing test labels overstated their assertions

The warped-photo browser test claimed that warped frames did not invent repetitions, but accepted any count up to five. The recorded fixture result was zero. The successor uses an exact-zero assertion and names it a controlled negative fixture; it does not call the photo warp a real squat.

The edge-bystander case only checked that tracking began. Its fixture includes a small second person that the lite engine historically missed. The successor records the one-detection limitation explicitly. Passing that case is not evidence that a detected bystander was rejected.

The prior network test allowed arbitrary same-origin requests and a broad CDN prefix, without checking method or body. The successor requires an a-priori exact static-resource list, GET, empty body, and no unapproved query or socket. Separate discriminating tests reject same-origin upload, alternate versions/paths, query-carried data, and undeclared egress. This strengthens the test; it is not a claim that the original source uploaded camera data.

The privacy test previously read `vision_bundle.mjs`, while the lab executed `vision_bundle.cjs`. The successor ties its scan to the actual import and checks both bundles. Required fake-camera assets no longer quietly turn a missing prerequisite into a skipped acceptance case. Asset routing in this suite remains a controlled test setup, not real-host startup proof.

The edited browser cases still require an actual exported build and focused Chromium run. Their source changes and policy-unit passes are not presented as browser passes.

## Lovable is a design donor with a different counting engine

The [Lovable research project](https://lovable.dev/projects/c1b16a13-34ff-4878-b798-750cc63aa595) is unpublished and has no database enabled. Current metadata supplies a preview address, but this audit did not establish a live hosted/device pass. Its current head differs from its functional candidate only by report/evidence files.

Its complete experimental flow is useful: introduction, squat demonstration, deliberate camera choice, manual alternative, setup/framing, readiness/countdown, pause guidance, finish, editable count, and a clearly simulated receipt. Those are design donors. Its sample ledger, test controls, surrounding prototype stores, and counting implementation are not production authority.

Unlike the accepted core, Lovable uses MediaPipe 1.0.1, a CPU worker by default, different squat geometry/thresholds, and a network monkeypatch introduced after historical telemetry attempts. Its main-thread fallback permits same-origin non-GET requests and the global patch is not restored on exit. That mechanism should not become the app's privacy boundary.

All 21 original Lovable synthetic tests passed when rerun. Three extra controlled probes exposed gaps:

- Replaying an identical timestamped three-rep sequence changed three to six because the engine lacks the accepted core's monotonic-input boundary.
- One deep erroneous landmark frame during an otherwise shallow movement created a counted rep; the bottom transition lacks the accepted core's dwell requirement.
- A same-position/size replacement after a 100 ms absence inherited the count. The displayed `lockSwitches` field stayed zero because it is initialized to zero and never updated. It is not identity evidence.

These are synthetic counterexamples, not measured failure rates on people. The reproduction tests intentionally assert the bad observations; they must never be added to the original 21 and advertised as 24 safety passes.

Three stored screenshot digests were verified and images inspected. The demonstration screen is a useful design reference. The live screenshot shows a fake green camera pattern, searching/paused tracking and approximately two frames per second; no human rep is proved. The receipt shows twelve explicitly user-entered squats and sample arithmetic, not twelve detected reps.

Decision for the next experiment: preserve the accepted technical core as the baseline, repair its concrete defects, and use Lovable's presentation through an explicit frozen design packet. Do not maintain two evolving algorithms under one ambiguous claim of parity. If an alternative algorithm is compared, label and score it as a separate candidate before selecting a successor.

## Minimum app integration after device proof

At development `0d3598d4`, the member route already performs `move -> enter -> review -> onRecord -> wsfContribute({goalId, attemptId, count}) -> confirmed receipt`. The camera can terminate at the editable entry step. An explicit Use this count action copies a valid local suggestion into the existing input; the member may correct it and then use the existing review/Record action.

The detector must never invoke Record, mint a parallel contribution, move the shared percentage, trigger Together completion, or claim independently verified exercise. A zero result stays zero and cannot become a fabricated contribution. Server replay keeps the original attempt/count; it is not a post-submission correction API.

The first integration can remain browser-only and scoped to one explicitly configured squat goal. The source's `activityGuideKey` is presentation guidance, not recording authority. Free-text unit/title matching is insufficient to decide that camera-counted squats qualify. Combined child-goal credit continues through the existing server rule; the camera does not submit a second parent increment or invent unit conversions.

Transient proposals must be bound to the current account, goal and local session generation. Route/account/goal changes discard them, and late callbacks cannot restore them. Keep model diagnostics, landmarks, imagery and timing traces out of Firebase, pending-contribution records, public displays and URLs.

## Station and native prerequisites

The aggregate `/kiosk/[goalId]` display is not the counter integration target. The newer `/station/[goalId]` route serves an enrolled queue turn, using `wsfStartTurn` and `wsfCompleteTurn`; the participant's phone can complete the same turn. The server owns the participant and attempt association.

Source inspection found a station count-lifetime concern: local `turnCount` is cleared after successful local complete/cancel actions, but the polling/assignment-change path does not visibly reset it. A phone-completed turn followed by a new participant could leave an old count available. This is a SOURCE-INSPECTED risk, not a reproduced running-app failure. It needs a targeted existing-station-owner test before adding asynchronous camera suggestions.

The current station completion payload contains station credentials and count, not an expected turn ID; the server resolves the active served turn. Station integration should evaluate an expected-turn precondition and reject late prior-turn actions. That is separate contribution-contract work, not a casual camera-module edit. Always clear proposals on turn changes and ignore late frames from a prior participant.

Native is a separate phase. The inspected app has no native camera/frame-processor dependency or camera plugin configuration, and its pending-attempt storage is browser `window.localStorage`. A native pose adapter and durable native pending recovery both require their own implementation and device proof. Browser/WebKit tests do not certify the native camera, iPhone Safari permission handling, GPU or application lifecycle.

## Parallel delivery and milestones

This thread owns the rep-counter audit, candidate selection, proof criteria and bounded research successor. The original W10 snapshot stays frozen; its old worker is not automatically reactivated. The current W3/W4 staging work and W9 product surfaces continue under the existing program control path. This audit creates no second integrator or polling schedule.

Use one writer for the counting/runtime files and a distinct reviewer for adversarial proof. Reference/UX work can advance independently against the frozen core contract. Reserve the contribution or station routes only when the counter has earned that integration step; do not let a research PR absorb current app work or move an issued design target.

The next milestones are:

1. Build and exercise the hardened research branch. Run the focused unit/controller/component tests, export the gated lab, and rerun the affected Chromium cases. Return exact source, dependency/model IDs, build receipt and test exclusions.
2. Provide an isolated HTTPS test surface for that exact candidate through a reviewed existing path. Verify actual model/WASM startup and camera failure/cleanup states. A preview URL without that receipt is not a test pass. Do not expose a production contribution route or publish the research as a finished capability.
3. Run the first iPhone Safari human smoke, then the actual kiosk camera/mount/browser. The same physical setup used at the event must be part of its evidence. Follow [DEVICE_TEST_PLAN.md](DEVICE_TEST_PLAN.md).
4. Calibrate on a small development sample, then freeze the candidate and score held-out sessions. Record misses and extra counts independently; a final total can hide cancelling errors. A valid squat missed during a tracking failure remains a miss.
5. After the supported-device gate passes and Devin reviews the result, introduce an optional camera suggestion into the existing member review flow. Run account/goal isolation, cancellation, unknown-result/replay, reduced-motion/focus, and confirmed-receipt regressions.
6. Add station consumption only after turn binding and reset behavior are proved. Pursue native camera and additional movements as separate measured capabilities.

The October 11 expo does not lower these criteria. Manual counting remains the usable participation path while the camera earns a narrowly supported optional role. No wearable, step-equivalent, movement-multiplier, automatic credit or form-scoring scope follows from this work.

## Technical research supporting the proof plan

Google's [web pose guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker/web_js) states that pose inference calls are synchronous and can block the UI thread. Measure inference time and inter-result gaps on the actual hardware; average frames per second can hide the gaps that invalidate repetitions. The [web setup guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/setup_web) lists Chrome and Safari but is not certification of this counter.

MDN's [track stop reference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaStreamTrack/stop) explains that explicit stop sets the track's readyState to ended without firing its ended event. Test each owned track's state and clear the video attachment. Another tab's camera ownership can keep the physical device indicator on.

Playwright's [browser documentation](https://playwright.dev/docs/browsers) distinguishes its patched WebKit build from branded Safari. Emulated phone viewports and a clean CI run do not replace physical iPhone camera testing.
